## Reid Hershel and Farah Oersted: The Melee Core

Reid Hershel is the protagonist, a young hunter from a quiet village in the world of Inferia. He wields a sword, his stats sit comfortably in the middle of the pack, and his attacks connect reliably. For anyone new to the series, he is the safest default — and community reports consistently recommend keeping him as your controlled character for the first several hours while you learn attack timing, blocking, and positioning.

Farah Oersted is Reid's childhood friend and a trained martial artist. She fights with her fists and feet, which means extremely short reach but very fast attack chains. She excels at chasing down enemies that like to reposition and at racking up long combo strings. If you enjoy aggressive, in-your-face play, she may quietly become your favorite character in the game.

Both of them are melee-first, so they share the same basic risk: standing in the wrong spot. The remaster's quality-of-life improvements make it easier to manage that, but the fundamentals still apply.

| Trait | Reid Hershel | Farah Oersted |
|---|---|---|
| Weapon | Sword | Fists and feet |
| Attack speed | Moderate | High |
| Reach | Short | Very short |
| Durability | Above average | Average |
| Learning curve | Easy | Moderate |
| Ideal player | Wants a safe, reliable lead | Wants fast, combo-heavy fights |

**Quick tips for the melee pair:**

- Keep Reid in the lead slot while you are still learning the battle flow.
- Switch to Farah when a fight rewards speed over raw damage.
- Level both evenly — the story regularly splits the party, and an underused character can leave you short-handed.
- Set your casters' tactics to stay back before you charge in with either one.

## Keele Zeibel, Meredy, and Chat: The Craymel Arts Users

Magic in Eternia comes from elemental spirits known as Craymels. Spellcasters capture them into Craymel Cages and channel their power through Craymel arts, which cover fire, water, wind, earth, ice, lightning, light, and darkness. Because elemental resistance is a real factor in this game, your casters are usually the fastest answer to a boss that shrugs off physical damage.

Keele Zeibel is a young scholar whose arts lean toward offense. He is the character to lean on when an enemy has an obvious elemental weakness, and he can put out serious damage in a short window — provided you keep him out of melee range.

Meredy is a young elf from Celestia, the second of the game's two intertwined worlds. She speaks in a distinctive way early in the story and brings elemental variety plus support options to the team. Players who enjoy planning a few steps ahead tend to gravitate toward her.

Chat is the youngest member of the group and the most flexible of the three. She needs some investment before she truly shines, but player experience suggests she rewards patience by fitting into a wide range of party builds.

## How Party Composition Works in the Aggressive Linear Motion Battle System

Eternia's battles are fought on a 2D plane. You directly control one hero, and the rest of the party acts according to the tactics you assign outside of combat. That means your real power over the team is twofold: who you bring, and how you tell them to behave.

A simple rule of thumb: control the character whose role best matches the fight, and let the AI handle the rest. The AI is generally competent when its tactics are set sensibly — attack freely for melee fighters, stay back and cast for your Craymel users, and defensive settings when a boss is charging a big attack.

| Situation | Recommended Lead | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| Learning the battle system | Reid Hershel | Balanced stats and forgiving combo timing |
| Chasing long combo chains | Farah Oersted | Fast attacks and quick repositioning |
| Boss with an elemental weakness | Keele Zeibel or Meredy | Craymel arts hit hard and hit fast |
| Experimenting with team builds | Chat | Flexible kit that supports many setups |
| Long dungeon runs | Any caster with support arts | Keeps the party steady between fights |

It is worth saying plainly: the "best" character depends on the encounter. Eternia is not a game where one party member carries every fight. Rotating your roster and matching elements to enemies will always outperform stubbornly sticking to one favorite.

## Tips for Building Your Party in Tales of Eternia Remastered

If you want a party that handles everything the two worlds throw at you, build around coverage rather than raw power. A balanced group — one sturdy melee lead, one fast attacker, and one or two Craymel arts users — will rarely run into a fight it cannot solve.

**Practical advice from the community:**

- Swap the character you control when a fight stalls; sometimes the problem is your lead, not your levels.
- Check enemy resistances before a boss rematch instead of grinding levels blindly.
- Keep at least one caster with healing or support options available for long dungeon stretches.
- Reassign tactics after every major party change — the AI does not adjust itself.
- Use the remaster's improved menus to re-gear quickly between story beats.

## FAQ About Tales of Eternia Remastered Playable Characters

**How many Tales of Eternia Remastered playable characters are there?**
The story centers on five main heroes — Reid Hershel, Farah Oersted, Keele Zeibel, Meredy, and Chat — and the playable roster grows as additional allies join the journey. You will not have everyone available from the opening hour.

**Who is the best character to start with?**
Reid Hershel. His balanced stats, reliable sword combos, and forgiving timing make him the easiest character to learn the Aggressive Linear Motion Battle System with. Once you are comfortable, switch to Farah for faster, more aggressive fights.

**Do the playable characters change in the remaster?**
No. The remaster keeps the original cast intact while adding enhanced visuals and quality-of-life improvements. What changes is how comfortable the game feels to play, not who you can recruit.

**Can I change party members during battle?**
You set your active party outside of combat. During a fight, your allies follow the tactics you assign, so adjusting those settings is the fastest way to change how your team behaves mid-battle.

Whether you favor Reid's steady swordsmanship, Farah's rapid strikes, or the elemental power of the Craymel arts users, Eternia rewards players who rotate their roster and think about matchups. Build a party that covers every element, and both worlds are yours to explore.
