## Tales of Eternia Remastered Steam Editions at a Glance

The PC release is a clean two-tier structure: the base game, and a Deluxe Edition that layers digital collectibles on top. The important detail for newcomers is that the playable content is identical in both. Story chapters, dungeons, artes, and battles do not change based on what you buy.

| Edition | Base game | Digital artbook | Digital soundtrack | Battle-themed bonus | Best suited to |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard Edition | Yes | No | No | No | First-time players who want the story and combat |
| Deluxe Edition | Yes | Yes | Yes — all 109 BGM tracks | Yes | Longtime fans and collectors |

Because nothing is gated behind the upgrade, the real question is whether you care about artbooks and music. If you have never finished the original, the standard version gives you the complete experience with no compromises.

The listing also confirms this is the first time Tales of Eternia has been remastered, which matters if you have only played it through older hardware or emulation. ## What the Deluxe Edition Actually Adds

The Deluxe Edition is built around three bonus categories, and the digital soundtrack is the headline item. According to the Tales of Eternia Remastered Steam listing, it contains every one of the 109 in-game BGM tracks — essentially the full score rather than a curated sampler.

| Deluxe item | What it is | Who it appeals to |
|---|---|---|
| Digital Artbook | Setting materials and illustrations from the game's world | Art, character, and worldbuilding fans |
| Digital Soundtrack | All 109 in-game background music tracks | Series music fans and collectors |
| Battle-themed bonus | Additional battle-related content referenced in the listing | Completionists and long-time players |

The artbook angle is worth a note for series historians. The copyright line credits Mutsumi Inomata alongside Bandai Namco Entertainment, so the artbook leans into the visual identity that defined the original release rather than generic promotional art.

It is also worth checking the store page directly, because bonus descriptions are sometimes truncated in search results and third-party listings. The [official Tales of Eternia Remastered Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3470960/Tales_of_Eternia_Remastered/) is the authoritative source for the current contents of each edition and any pre-release adjustments.

### Which edition should you pick?

- **Choose Standard** if you play once, finish, and move on — you lose zero gameplay.
- **Choose Deluxe** if you already own the original and want a preservation-style package with the score and art.
- **Wait for user reviews** if you're unsure about remaster quality; the listing showed no user reviews at the time of writing, so community impressions were still unavailable.

## PC System Requirements and Performance Targets

The Tales of Eternia Remastered Steam system requirements are unusually modest, and the minimum and recommended hardware are nearly identical. The difference sits almost entirely in the target graphics preset rather than the components themselves.

| Component | Minimum | Recommended |
|---|---|---|
| OS | Windows 11 | Windows 11 |
| Processor | Intel Core i3-8100 / AMD Ryzen 3 3100 | Intel Core i3-8100 / AMD Ryzen 3 3100 |
| Memory | 4 GB RAM | 4 GB RAM |
| Graphics | GTX 650 Ti (1 GB) / Radeon HD 7770 (2 GB) / Intel Arc A310 (4 GB) | Same |
| DirectX | Version 12 | Version 12 |
| Storage | 8 GB available space | 8 GB available space |
| Stated target | 1080p / 60fps at Low settings | 1080p / 60fps at High settings |

Three takeaways stand out. First, 8 GB of storage is tiny by modern standards — this is a 2D remaster, not a rebuilt 3D production. Second, 4 GB of RAM is enough, which makes the game viable on older laptops and budget desktops. Third, both tiers list the same GPU options, so buying a new graphics card specifically for this release makes little sense.

The listing does warn that frame rates can dip in graphics-intensive scenes and notes that DirectX 12 requires a GPU with 4 GB of VRAM. If your card sits below that, verify your setup before launch rather than after.

## Gameplay, Story, and Quality-of-Life Upgrades

The narrative premise is unchanged from the original: Eternia is split into two isolated realms, Inferia and Celestia, and their collision — the Great Fall — threatens both. In the remote Inferian village of Rasheans, Reid Hershel and his childhood friend Farah Oersted meet a mysterious girl named Meredy, and the search for her origins becomes a mission to save two worlds using the elemental Greater Craymels.

Combat still runs on the Aggressive Linear Motion Battle System, or A-LMBS. That means fast 2D battles built around arte usage, extended combos, and coordinating each party member's strengths rather than menu-driven turn order. If you have played later entries in the series, the pace will feel familiar.

| Quality-of-life feature | What it does |
|---|---|
| Destination markers | On-screen guidance pointing you toward your next objective |
| Encounter toggle | Lets you disable random enemy encounters |
| High-speed auto-battle | Speeds up routine fights |
| Graphics and SFX toggle | Switches between remastered and original presentation |

That last toggle is the most interesting addition for veterans. Being able to swap between updated visuals and the original look means the remaster respects nostalgia instead of overwriting it — a smart choice for a game whose identity is tied to its era.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Tales of Eternia Remastered Steam

**When does the game unlock?**

The Tales of Eternia Remastered Steam page lists a release date of October 15, 2026, with an unlock time of 3:00 PM PDT. Because of time zones, that translates to October 16 for European players. Wishlisting the game in the Steam app is the simplest way to get a notification the moment it goes live.

**Does the Deluxe Edition include extra gameplay?**

No. Based on the published listing, the Deluxe Edition adds a digital artbook, a digital soundtrack with all 109 BGM tracks, and a battle-themed bonus. None of the story, dungeons, or combat systems are exclusive to it.

**Can I play it on Steam Deck?**

Yes. The game carries a Verified rating for Steam Deck compatibility, meaning it runs with controller support and readable text without manual tweaking.

**Is there multiplayer or co-op?**

The store page lists single-player as the only mode. There is no co-op or online multiplayer component, which matches the structure of the original game.

For a two-world JRPG that never officially reached modern platforms, this release is about accessibility as much as visuals. Whether you go Standard or Deluxe, you are getting the same journey to Eternia — the only choice is how much of its art and music you want to keep afterward.
