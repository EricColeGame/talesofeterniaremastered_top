## Why Tales of Eternia Remastered Achievements Take Longer Than You Think

Chasing 100% completion in a classic JRPG is one of the most satisfying — and most time-consuming — projects a player can take on, and the Tales of Eternia Remastered achievements list sits right at the center of that grind. Whether you are reuniting with Reid, Farah, Keele, and Meredy or meeting them for the first time, the Tales of Eternia Remastered achievements are what turn a nostalgic story run into a structured, checklisted campaign.

Tales of Eternia originally launched on the original PlayStation in 2000 and reached North America under the title Tales of Destiny II before being ported to the PSP a few years later. A remaster brings that journey back with modern presentation and, for completionists, a brand-new achievement and trophy list attached to it.

That matters because JRPG achievement lists are rarely just "finish the story." They reward system mastery — the Overlimit gauge, Blast Calibers, Craymel Arts, cooking, the arena, and the bestiary — and they usually hide a few entries behind content you can permanently lose access to. Planning your route before the opening hour is the difference between one clean run and three painful replays.

## The Main Achievement Categories You'll Be Working Through

Most Tales remasters build their lists from the same handful of buckets. If you know the buckets, you know roughly what you are signing up for.

| Category | What it typically asks for | Missable risk | Typical effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| Story progression | Clear each major dungeon and chapter | None | Low |
| Battle system | Win a set number of battles, land long combos, trigger Blast Calibers | None | Medium |
| Character growth | Learn every arte for each party member, develop Craymels | Low | High |
| Side content | Mini-games, arena ranks, sub-events, cooking recipes | High | High |
| Collection | Bestiary entries, item and weapon compendiums, titles | Medium | High |
| Difficulty and post-game | Clear on the hardest setting, defeat optional super-bosses | Low | Very High |

The pattern is consistent across the series: the story achievements arrive on their own, and the last ten percent of the list eats the majority of your playtime. Player experience with previous Tales remasters suggests the completionist tail — artes, bestiary, arena, super-bosses — is where most people stall out.

A practical takeaway: treat the "free" story achievements as a reward, not a goal. Your real planning should focus on the four categories marked Medium or High above.

## Missable Achievements: Where 100% Runs Fall Apart

This is the section that saves you a replay. Classic Tales games are packed with timed events, one-time-only scenes, and NPC conversations that quietly disappear the moment the plot moves forward. A remaster usually preserves those quirks rather than fixing them, so the same traps apply.

| Story stage | What to check before advancing | What you lose if you skip it |
|---|---|---|
| Opening hours and first region | Talk to every NPC twice, buy limited early-shop stock, finish village sub-events | Early side scenes and small item chains |
| Mid-game world shift | Clear optional dungeons, arena ranks, and any recipe or item vendors | Recipes and gear that get harder or impossible to obtain later |
| Late game / point of no return | Sweep every side quest, mini-game, and optional boss | Entire quest chains close permanently |
| Final dungeon and post-game | Save in a separate slot before the finale | Forces a New Game Plus restart |

Community reports from earlier versions of the game consistently flag the same habits as the difference-makers:

- **Keep a rotating save file.** One save per region or chapter, never overwrite your oldest slot.
- **Talk to NPCs after every story beat.** Dialogue changes constantly, and some events only trigger on a second or third pass.
- **Never sell unique items.** If an item has a story name or comes from a boss, it stays in your inventory.
- **Screenshot your checklist.** Long RPGs blur together after twenty hours; your notes will not.
- **Finish optional dungeons when you find them.** The moment a dungeon is "out of the way," it is one patch away from being locked.

If you only follow one rule from this guide, make it the rotating save file. It costs nothing and it converts a full replay into a two-hour cleanup.

## A Step-by-Step Roadmap to 100% Completion

Here is a route that works for most Tales remaster lists without requiring you to spoil the story on your first pass.

| Step | Focus | Why it comes in this order |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Play the story on Normal, but talk to everyone | You get the story achievements while scouting missables |
| 2 | Before each point of no return, sweep side content | Prevents the most common permanent lockouts |
| 3 | Start a dedicated battle-grind session | Combo and arte counters fall fastest with an endgame party |
| 4 | Clear optional dungeons and super-bosses | Needs high levels and your best gear |
| 5 | New Game Plus on the hardest difficulty | Carryover stats make the difficulty trophy manageable |
| 6 | Final mop-up run | Bestiary, titles, cooking, and anything you missed |

Step 5 is the one people underestimate. Many Tales remasters attach a trophy to the highest difficulty, and the cleanest way to earn it is a second run with carried-over levels and equipment rather than a fresh, brutal playthrough. If the remaster offers difficulty-related assists, check whether they disable trophies before you lean on them — the rules vary between releases, and player experience with similar ports has been mixed.

For official news on the remaster, patches, and platform availability, keep an eye on [Bandai Namco Entertainment's official site](https://www.bandainamcoent.com/), which is the most reliable source for updates that can affect achievement behavior.

## Efficient Grinding for Battle, Arte, and Collection Goals

Grinding in Tales games is not about fighting more — it is about fighting the *right* encounters with the right setup. Overlimit, Blast Calibers, and Craymel progression all reward specific play patterns.

| What you're grinding | Best approach | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Levels and gald | Enemy-dense corridors in late-game areas or the post-game dungeon | Highest experience per minute |
| Arte usage counts | Spam the cheapest arte on weak, respawning enemies | Low cost, fast animation, no risk |
| Blast Caliber triggers | Fight large groups so the Overlimit gauge fills quickly | Gauge gain scales with hits taken and dealt |
| Long combos | Pick a high-HP enemy and set party AI to defend | Keeps the target alive long enough |
| Cooking recipes | Cook after every battle and buy recipes in every town | Recipe availability is tied to story progress |
| Bestiary entries | Fight everything once per region before moving on | Some monsters never respawn later |

A few habits that make the grind dramatically shorter:

- **Rotate your party.** Benched characters do not learn artes. If an achievement tracks every character's skill list, keep everyone active.
- **Use cooking as free healing.** It doubles as recipe progress and cuts item costs.
- **Batch your goals.** A single session can advance levels, arte counts, combo records, and gald at the same time.
- **Check for remaster quality-of-life options.** Many modern remasters ship with battle speed, encounter rate, or experience toggles. If this one does, they are fair game for cleanup — just confirm they do not void anything.

## Difficulty Trophies, New Game Plus, and Post-Game Content

The endgame is where Tales of Eternia Remastered achievements get genuinely hard. Super-bosses and the hardest difficulty setting are usually the last two items on anyone's list.

| Goal | Recommended approach | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hardest difficulty clear | New Game Plus with carried-over stats | Turns a wall into a challenge |
| Optional super-bosses | Max level, best weapons, full item stock | Expect long fights and status effects |
| All artes learned | Rotate party members from hour one | Retrofitting this later is brutal |
| Bestiary and item compendium | Track region by region | Missed entries may need another run |
| Titles and arena ranks | Clear arena tiers as soon as they unlock | Higher tiers scale with your level |

Two more points worth internalizing. First, "post-game" in a Tales game is real content, not a victory lap — the optional dungeon and super-bosses are typically tuned for a party that has already finished the story. Second, if an achievement asks you to defeat a boss without using items or under a restriction, do it on a New Game Plus run where your stats give you a buffer. Community reports consistently name restriction-based fights as the single most frustrating entries on these lists.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Tales of Eternia Remastered Achievements

**How long does it take to unlock all Tales of Eternia Remastered achievements?**
It depends heavily on how much you plan. A blind story run plus a New Game Plus cleanup is the standard route, and completionists should expect a substantial time investment — a full playthrough, a second run for difficulty, and several hours of grinding for artes, bestiary entries, and combo records.

**Are any achievements missable?**
Yes, that is the norm for this series. Timed events, one-time NPC scenes, and optional dungeons that close when the story advances are the usual culprits. Keep multiple saves and sweep side content before every point of no return.

**Do I need New Game Plus to get 100%?**
Usually, yes — or at least it is by far the easiest way. A second run with carried-over levels is the standard method for clearing the hardest difficulty and for finishing any collectibles you missed.

**Can I earn everything on the easiest difficulty?**
Not typically. Most Tales remasters tie at least one achievement to the highest difficulty setting. Play the story on Normal for comfort, then do a dedicated difficulty run in New Game Plus.

**Is it worth chasing 100% at all?**
If you enjoy the battle system, absolutely. The Craymel and arte systems only reveal their depth when you are forced to use them properly, and the achievement list is a good excuse to see content most players never touch.
